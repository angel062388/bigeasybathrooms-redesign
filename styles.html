Heading treatments

Sand motif, and seven ways to highlight a keyword

Each option below is live. Press Replay to watch the animation again. The logo is untouched throughout, and every option switches itself off for anyone whose device asks for reduced motion.

  • Sand#C7B29A
  • Sand light#DCCDB8
  • Taupe#AE9E90
  • Brand navy#04223D
  • Cream ground#F8F6F2
One hard limit before you choose. Sand used as text colour on the cream ground measures 1.90:1 contrast. The accessible minimum is 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large headings, so sand lettering fails on both counts, and this audience is the least able to absorb that. Sand used as a background behind navy text measures 7.89:1 and passes at the strictest level. That is why six of the seven options below put the sand behind or under the words rather than in them.

Underline sweep

Option 1

Let us help you reimagine your bathroom in New Orleans

Contrast safe · text stays navy

A rounded sand bar grows out from the left under the keyword. The words themselves stay full navy, so nothing about legibility changes. This is the closest match to what TurnKey Pool Builders does, rebuilt in Big Easy's colour.

Highlighter block

Option 2

Walk-in tubs and barrier-free showers built for safety

7.89:1 · passes AAA

A sand block wipes in behind the words like a highlighter pen. The strongest option for this audience: it is the most visible from a distance, and it is the one that actually uses the sand colour at full strength. My pick.

Drawn underline

Option 3

Your local NOLA renovation specialists

Contrast safe · text stays navy

A slightly uneven line draws itself under the phrase, left to right, as though done by hand. Warmer and less corporate than a straight bar. Costs one small inline graphic per heading.

Word lift

Option 4

Where luxury meets comfort

Contrast safe · text stays navy

The keywords rise into place one after another, then a sand underline lands on the last one. The most movement of any option here. Worth weighing against the audience: it is the one most likely to feel busy.

Gradient lettering

Option 5

Reimagine your bathroom this year

1.90:1 · fails WCAG on cream

The keyword fills with a sand gradient, left to right. It is included because it is the look people usually mean by "highlighted", and because you should see it before ruling it out. On the cream ground the letters are close to invisible for anyone with reduced vision. Only usable on the dark navy band, which is Option 6.

Glow, on dark bands only

Option 6

Ready to start your bathroom remodel?

Safe on navy only

Sand glows against the navy band, with a soft pulse that keeps breathing. This is the one place light sand lettering earns its contrast, because the ground behind it is dark. Do not carry it onto the cream sections.

Brackets

Option 7

Serving Metairie and Slidell

Contrast safe · text stays navy

Sand brackets slide in and close around the phrase. Quiet, and it reads well at small sizes where an underline can get lost.

What I would actually ship. Option 2 as the main device on cream sections, Option 6 on the navy bands, and nothing else. Mixing more than two treatments across one page makes the highlighting stop meaning anything. Options 1 and 3 are good alternates if the block feels too heavy.
